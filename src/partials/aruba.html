What to Consider When Choosing an Aruba Solution

The right configuration should be selected for the actual operating environment, not simply by port count or price. Key factors include the number of users, facility type, Wi-Fi client density, the number of PoE devices and total PoE budget, uplink requirements and bandwidth, VLAN design, the need for Aruba Central and NAC, support level, and plans for future growth. That is why every solution should begin with an assessment.

F.A.Q.

Clear answers before we start

  • Which Aruba solution is right for an office?

    It depends on the number of users and devices, the size and layout of the premises, and the required Wi-Fi coverage. We determine the optimal combination of switches and access points during the assessment.

  • How does Aruba Central benefit a business?

    It provides cloud-based network management: all switches and access points are visible and configurable from a single interface. The benefits are especially clear across multiple sites - issues can be identified earlier and administration becomes simpler.

  • What is required for high-quality enterprise Wi-Fi?

    It starts with proper design: coverage tailored to the space, sufficient capacity for user density, reliable roaming, and appropriate segmentation. For business-critical environments, a Wi-Fi survey is recommended to account for obstacles that can affect the signal.

  • When is ClearPass or NAC needed?

    When you need to control which users and devices can connect to the network: user and device authentication, different access rights for different groups, and separate scenarios for guests and IoT. The more devices and security requirements you have, the greater the benefit.

  • How should PoE switches be selected?

    By calculating the PoE budget: the number of devices, the power draw of each device, and the switch's total power capacity with adequate headroom. We perform this calculation during the design phase.

  • Does NEOLA help with migrations to HPE Aruba?

    Yes. We support the entire transition - from assessment and design through deployment, testing, and ongoing support - and plan the work to minimize impact on business operations.
