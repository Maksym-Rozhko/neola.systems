F.A.Q.

Clear answers before we start

  • Why does a business need Veeam?

    To protect data and restore it quickly after an outage, human error, hardware failure, or ransomware attack. Veeam protects virtual, physical, cloud, and Microsoft 365 environments and supports granular recovery of files, machines, or services.

  • What can Veeam protect?

    Virtual machines (VMware, Hyper-V), physical servers and workstations (Windows, Linux), cloud workloads, and Microsoft 365 data such as email, files, and documents. We identify which systems and data are critical during the assessment.

  • Do you need backup for Microsoft 365?

    In many cases, yes. Microsoft is responsible for service availability, but recovering your data after deletion, user error, or ransomware often remains the company's responsibility, especially beyond standard retention periods.

  • What is immutable backup?

    It is a backup that cannot be modified or deleted for a defined period. Even if an attacker gains access to the infrastructure, the protected copy cannot be overwritten, making immutability an important part of ransomware resilience.

  • Can Veeam help after a ransomware attack?

    Yes. Veeam can restore data from clean backups. If a valid recovery point is available - especially an immutable one - data can be restored to a state before encryption. Veeam does not prevent the attack itself; it provides a way to recover afterward.

  • What determines the cost of Veeam?

    The number of workloads and Microsoft 365 users, data volume and growth, retention period, storage choice, the need for offsite and immutable copies, RPO/RTO targets, and the support level. We assess these factors first and then define the configuration and budget.

  • When is backup enough, and when do you need a disaster recovery scenario?

    Backup may be sufficient when the business can tolerate recovery taking several hours or days and can accept some data loss between backup points. If the time required to restore specific services is also critical, a disaster recovery scenario should be planned as well. We determine the right approach during the RPO/RTO assessment.
